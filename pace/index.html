The PACE Framework · Free

AI didn't 10x you. Here's why.

The bottleneck was never the model's speed. It's that your attention is single-threaded and your work doesn't accumulate.

You got a coding agent that writes a module in four minutes, and your week did not get five times more productive. That's not a disappointing model. It's a bottleneck that moved.

The slow part used to be typing. Now the slow part is you — reading the output, deciding the next thing, answering a question that arrived while you were somewhere else. An agent that finishes in four minutes and then waits ninety for you to look at it is a four-minute agent on paper and a ninety-minute agent in your calendar.

The second half is worse and quieter. Most people run their tenth task exactly the way they ran their first: same explaining, same corrections, same context typed out again from memory. Nothing accumulates. You are fast now, but you are fast in a straight line, and you will still be going the same speed in six months.

PACE is two laws for those two problems. Parallel Action for the first, Compounding Effort for the second.

The two laws

Law 1

Act in Parallel

Your agents can run concurrently. You cannot. Five of them can be working right now and the only thing stopping that is that four of them are waiting on you to read something.

So stop being the thing they wait on. Give a task enough context to run for an hour without you, get every blocking question answered in one message before the work starts, and let it proceed on stated assumptions instead of stopping at the first fork.

The skill this rewards isn't prompting. It's dispatch — cutting work into pieces that can run unattended, and briefing them well enough that they do.

Metric: agent-hours per human-hour. If it's hovering near one, you're supervising, not dispatching.

Law 2

Compound Effort

If your tenth task isn't faster than your first, you don't have a system. You have a habit. A habit you're very good at and that will never get better on its own.

Context is an asset. The conventions you explained, the mistake you corrected, the shape of a brief that worked — every one of those is either written down where the next run picks it up, or it evaporates when the session closes.

So correct the system, not the output. When something comes out wrong because the context was missing, fixing the answer buys you one task. Fixing the context buys you every task after it.

Metric: is task ten cheaper than task one? If not, nothing you did in between was a system.

Why it takes both

Parallel without compounding is chaos. Six agents running, six sets of output to reconcile, the same corrections issued six times, and a person at the middle of it burning out inside a month. Volume with no memory is just a faster way to make the same mess.

Compounding without parallel is a beautiful system operated by one slow human. The templates are good, the context files are current, the conventions are documented — and everything still moves at the speed of one person reading one thing at a time.

Together they change shape. Work runs wide, and every run leaves the next one cheaper. That's throughput that keeps climbing while you're asleep, which is the only version of this that ever felt like 10x.
