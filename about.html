So what is this, exactly?

A suite of apps for building with AI — the tools I built to ship my own work, after 25 years of building for the web.

Wynter.ai is a suite of apps for building with AI — planning the build, writing the spec, shaping the brand, keeping the tasks moving. Each one started as something I built for my own projects because the alternative was a blank page and a lot of guessing.

They are separate apps on purpose. A single tool that claims to do all of it ends up doing none of it well — so BuildThisFunnel only cares about funnels and DoTheseTasks only cares about keeping the work moving. What holds them together isn't a mega-app, it's one account.

Who's behind it

I'm Wynter Jones, and I've been building for the web for more than 25 years. Recent work includes ClickFunnels Classic, Udio, and MarketingSecretsAI, and I work alongside a lot of the marketers who set the pace in this space — Russell Brunson among them.

That's the background this suite came out of. Every app here started in a real project where I needed the thing, couldn't buy it, and built it anyway — then used it long enough to find out where it was wrong and fixed that.

I work small and ship often: narrow tools, in front of people early, sharpened by whatever actually breaks. It's an unglamorous way to build and it's the only one I've seen hold up — a tool nobody has used yet is a guess, however good the plan was.

That's also why the free tools, the training, and the checklists sit out front rather than behind a wall. The fastest way to learn whether something works is to watch people use it.

One account, on purpose

Every app runs on a single Wynter account. One login, one subscription, one shared credit balance — not a separate signup and a separate invoice per app. Sign up once and everything is already open when you get there.

That's also why the free plan is genuinely free rather than a trial with a countdown. You should be able to use the tools, get something out of it, and only pay when you hit the part that's worth paying for.

Open source too

Not everything is a product. Farmwork is an open, free workflow framework for agentic development with Claude Code and other AI coding tools — the same harness I use to build everything here. OpenPaw is a self-hosted AI workbench: you describe the tool you need, agents build it as a real running service, and the whole thing stays on your own machine.

Both are free, both are open to read, and both work with the Wynter account you already have.

How I build

Small products, shipped fast, then sharpened. I'd rather put a narrow tool in front of people this month than a broad one next year, and I'd rather fix the thing people actually hit than the thing I imagined they'd hit. Most of what exists here does so because a real project of mine needed it first.
