About

I build the tools I needed.

Wynter.ai is a suite of apps for building with AI, on one account and one price, after 25 years of building for the web.

Every app here started in a real project where I needed the thing, couldn't buy it, and built it anyway — then used it long enough to find out where it was wrong and fixed that.

That's the whole reason this page exists. A tool nobody has run is a guess with a landing page attached. These came out of shipping: the apps, the harness underneath them, and a lot of ordinary days finding out which parts survive a normal working week.

Who's behind it

I'm Wynter Jones, and I've been building for the web for more than 25 years. Recent work includes ClickFunnels Classic and MarketingSecretsAI, and I work alongside a lot of the marketers who set the pace in this space — Russell Brunson among them.

I work small and ship often: narrow tools, in front of people early, sharpened by whatever actually breaks. It's an unglamorous way to build and it's the only one I've seen hold up — a tool nobody has used yet is a guess, however good the plan was.

What I've built

Wynter.ai is a suite of apps for building with AI — planning the build, writing the spec, shaping the brand, keeping the tasks moving. They are separate apps on purpose: a single tool that claims to do all of it ends up doing none of it well, so BuildThisFunnel only cares about funnels and DoTheseTasks only cares about keeping the work moving.

Farmwork is an open, free workflow framework for agentic development with Claude Code and other AI coding tools — the same harness I use to build everything here. OpenPaw is a self-hosted AI workbench: you describe the tool you need, agents build it as a real running service, and the whole thing stays on your own machine. Both are free, both are open to read, and both work with the Wynter.ai account you already have.

One account, one price

Every app runs on a single Wynter.ai account. One login, one subscription, one shared credit balance — not a separate signup and a separate invoice per app. Sign up once and everything is already open when you get there.

That's also why the free plan is genuinely free rather than a trial with a countdown. You should be able to use the apps, get something out of it, and only pay when you hit the part that's worth paying for. Pro is $28 a month, and AI usage is bought as credits rather than bundled into the subscription.

The method behind it

PACE is two laws for working with coding agents: act in parallel, so your attention stops being the thing they wait on, and compound effort, so the tenth task is faster than the first. It's how I actually work, and it's free — the two laws, the templates and the protocol are on this site in full, with no account and no email form.

That's the same reason the free features, the training, and the checklists sit out front rather than behind a wall. The fastest way to learn whether something works is to watch people use it.
